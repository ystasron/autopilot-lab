Autopilot Lab

Self-driving city simulation

GitHub repository Simulation live

A small city with traffic lights, crosswalks and sidewalks. The green car drives itself through the grid while other cars and pedestrians share the streets. It is a 4 × 3 grid of intersections, each with its own signal timing, crosswalks and corner sidewalks. Everything runs in your browser, and the source is on GitHub.

    How it drives

    Sense

    Five overlays show what the green car perceives: LiDAR points, the camera cone with labelled detections, its lane corridor and the planned path. Red LiDAR points are things that move; blue ones are static road edges.

    Predict

    Every car and person is projected 3.5 seconds ahead from their current heading and speed. People waiting at a kerb are treated as stationary until their light changes. Purple and pink lines show these forecasts.

    Plan

    Routing runs Dijkstra over 48 intersection states, then adds a penalty for roads with slow or queued cars and reroutes when that saves distance. It signals turns, slows for corners and picks stop-or-go on a yellow from its braking distance versus time left.

    Act

    Six candidate speeds are simulated along the planned path against the forecasts. The fastest one with no predicted conflict wins, so it yields to people and oncoming cars only when a conflict is actually forecast. On a red it glides to arrive as the light turns green.

    Explain

    The Reasoning panel logs each change of intent with the cause, distance and time to conflict, and a risk meter shows how close the preferred speed came to a predicted collision.

    Things to try

    Experiments

    Pin a destination

    Click anywhere on the map with “pins destination” selected. Watch the Reasoning log: it plans the route, then may reroute if queues build on the way.

    Flip a light

    Set Click to “flips light” and click an intersection to shift its signal phase. Place a red in the car’s path and see it slow early to time the green.

    Add pedestrians

    Press “Add 10 people” and drive through crowded crosswalks. Pink forecast lines show who is predicted to step into the road, and the car yields only when a conflict is predicted.

    Night mode

    Turns on headlights, street lamps and glowing signals. Driving logic does not change, but it is the clearest way to see the signals and the LiDAR layer.

    Take over

    Turn autopilot off and drive with the arrow keys: up accelerates, down brakes, left and right steer. The Collisions and Road exits counters keep score.

    About this project

    Open source

    Autopilot Lab is a single-page simulation made of three plain files: index.html for the layout, style.css for the look and sim.js for the city, the traffic and the driving logic. There is no build step and no dependencies. The other cars follow simpler rules (cautious, normal and hurried drivers), while the green car uses a predictive planner that forecasts other road users, tests candidate speeds and explains its choices. To change how it drives, start with the think() function in sim.js.

    Source code, issues and updates: github.com/ystasron/autopilot-lab

    Autopilot Lab

    A browser-based self-driving car simulation in a small city.

    • Live perception overlays — LiDAR sweep, camera FOV, labelled detections
    • Predictive planning: forecasts everyone 3.5 s ahead and tests speeds before choosing
    • Reasoning panel that explains each decision as it happens
    • Real routing: Dijkstra over 48 intersection states, no U-turns
    • Rule-following traffic with cautious, normal and hurried drivers
    • Pedestrians, night mode, and Follow / Free camera modes
